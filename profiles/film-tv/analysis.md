# Evaluation goal

Evaluate whether a piece of film or television writing gives readers timely, credible, and materially useful insight into the industry, creative practice, commercial performance, or cultural impact of film and television.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Systemic.** Major industry restructurings (studio mergers, streamer strategy pivots, guild strikes, AI-and-labor disputes), landmark box-office records that reset market expectations, sweeping awards-season upsets with long-term consequences, or films/shows that fundamentally shift the cultural conversation.

- **7-8: Important.** Major festival premieres and awards (Cannes, Venice, Berlin, Sundance, TIFF, Emmys, Oscars), high-profile casting, directing, or showrunner appointments with clear directional signals, significant production deals, or critical reception that meaningfully shifts discourse around a filmmaker, showrunner, or franchise.

- **5-6: Useful.** Credible and concrete production reports, in-depth reviews that engage seriously with form and craft, box-office and streaming performance analysis with meaningful baselines, industry interviews about craft decisions, or documentation of institutional policies (streaming windows, residuals, ratings) that affect a defined creative community.

- **3-4: Low value.** Routine casting announcements, predictable franchise updates, superficial box-office roundups without context, fluff celebrity interviews, or social-media-driven controversies that lack evidence or lasting consequence.

- **0-2: Noise.** Unsubstantiated rumors, clickbait "first reactions" without substantive criticism, promotional hype disguised as reporting, or content with no discernible connection to actual films, shows, filmmakers, or the industry.

# Evaluation guidance

Consider the magnitude and breadth of the event, source quality (trade press vs. tabloid, primary reporting vs. aggregation), timeliness, affected groups (directors, writers, actors, studios, streamers, exhibitors, audiences), and whether the item provides concrete figures (box office, budgets, streaming hours, ratings, awards counts) with meaningful baselines (previous films, franchise norms, year-over-year comparisons). Distinguish reported facts from critical opinions. Do not reward a dramatic box-office opening without considering the film's marketing spend and screen count, and do not treat social-media virality as evidence of artistic or commercial importance.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
